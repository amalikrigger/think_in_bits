Shortcuts, and what they cost you

These get the right answer fast, and they are worth knowing. But read the warning under each one before you use it in a written answer, because Cambridge marks your method, not just your final line.

▶

Factor a quadratic in less than a minute

ChillMath · Short, 43 seconds ShortSlide and divide

The catch: the second line he writes is not equal to the expression you started with. He multiplies it by 2, factors the easier version, then divides back at the end. It works, and he does not skip that last step, but if you write that middle line in an exam you cannot justify it and you will lose method marks. Use it to check an answer you already have. Do not use it to write one.

The homework set. Nineteen questions, answers on the same page, mark yourself and bring in anything that did not come out.

A note on the endpoints. None of the three inequality shorts explains why x = 1 is sometimes included in the answer and sometimes not. Here is the rule. If the question uses < or >, the critical values are out, because at those values the expression equals exactly zero and zero is not less than zero. If it uses ≤ or ≥, they are in. Slide 8 of the lesson covers this properly.
