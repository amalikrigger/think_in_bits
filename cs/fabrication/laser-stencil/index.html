Laser: Stencil or Engrave

Engrave it, cut it out, paint through it, or stamp it on a T-shirt

  • Grades 7 to 12
  • Time 1 to 2 class periods, plus your turn on the laser
  • Difficulty Beginner
  • Grade Optional

A laser cutter is a beam of light strong enough to burn wood. Point it at a picture and it can draw that picture into a coaster, cut it out as a keychain, turn it into a stencil for a T-shirt, or carve it into a rubber stamp. Mr. Krigger shows you how the Glowforge works. Your job is to show up with a design: one of the 35 on this page, a picture you found, or something you drew. He runs the laser, and you leave with something you made. Work alone or team up with one or two friends.

This one is optional. Do it and it counts toward your grade. Skip it and it is marked Exempt, so it cannot hurt you. Due Friday, November 20.

What you'll learn

Safety rules: the laser

Short list, no exceptions. Break one on purpose and you lose laser access.

  • An adult is in the room whenever the laser runs, and the lid stays closed.
  • Someone watches it the whole time. Nobody walks away from a running laser.
  • No vinyl and no PVC, ever. Mr. Krigger picks or checks every material that goes in.
  • Erasers: pink rubber only. White or clear "plastic" erasers are usually vinyl, and vinyl never goes in the laser.
  • A flame that keeps going or smoke that will not clear: tell Mr. Krigger right away.
Why the vinyl rule matters. Vinyl and PVC have chlorine in them. Burning them makes a gas that hurts your lungs and eats the metal inside the machine. That is why nothing goes in the laser unless Mr. Krigger says so.

How the laser reads your picture

There are two kinds of picture files, and the laser treats them very differently.

A PNG or JPG is made of tiny colored dots. The laser can only shade those dots in, row by row, like a printer. That is engraving.

An SVG is made of lines. The laser can follow a line all the way around a shape, like tracing with a pencil. That is how it cuts. So if you want anything cut out, you need an SVG.

Why stencils need bridges

A stencil is a sheet with holes in it. You lay it on a shirt and dab paint over it. Paint goes through the holes and nowhere else, so the holes become your picture.

Here is the catch. Think about the letter O. To cut an O out of a sheet, the laser cuts around the outside and around the inside. The middle of the O is now a loose piece with nothing holding it, so it falls out. Paint goes straight through where it used to be, and your O becomes a dot.

The fix is to leave a couple of tiny strips uncut so the middle stays attached. Those strips are called bridges. The loose middle piece they save is called an island.

See it with one word

The rule: make every bridge at least 2 mm wide. Thinner ones tear when you peel the stencil. Engravings, cut-outs and stamps do not need bridges at all.

Step 1: Turn your picture into an SVG

Only needed if you want it cut (a cut-out or a stencil). Engravings and stamps work straight from a PNG or JPG. Pick whichever way is easier for you.

Quick · in your browser

  1. Go to pngtosvg.com.
  2. Drop your picture in.
  3. Download the SVG it makes.

More control · Inkscape

  1. File, then Import your picture.
  2. Click it, then Path, then Trace Bitmap.
  3. Pick Brightness cutoff, slide Threshold until it looks right, then Apply.
  4. Drag the trace aside and delete the original picture.
Checkpoint: an SVG that looks like your picture, in solid black shapes.

Step 2: Size it, and check for islands

  1. Make it 100 mm wide or less. In Inkscape, switch the unit box at the top to mm, click the lock, and type the width. Not using Inkscape? Mr. Krigger can resize it in the Glowforge app.
  2. Making a stencil? Zoom in and look for any shape sitting inside another shape, like the middle of an O. Each of those needs a bridge. In Inkscape, draw a 2 mm wide rectangle across it, select both, then click Path, then Difference.
  3. Save it with your section and initials:
HS-JK-laser.svg
Checkpoint: 100 mm or smaller and named with your section and initials. Stencils: every island has a bridge.

Making a stamp

A stamp is an engraving turned inside out. Instead of burning your design in, the laser burns everything around your design away, so your design is left standing up. Paint goes on the raised part, and the raised part is what prints.

  • Bring a pink rubber eraser, the big block kind. Never a white or clear one.
  • Bold and simple wins. Thick lines and chunky shapes stamp cleanly. Thin lines fill in with paint.
  • Words come out backwards when you stamp, like a mirror. You do not have to fix it. Tell Mr. Krigger it is a stamp and he flips it before he engraves it.
  • Small is fine. Your design has to fit on the eraser, so think 2 to 5 cm.
Mr. Krigger tests the eraser first. Erasers are not sold for lasers, so he runs a tiny test on each new kind before anyone engraves one.

Vocabulary

TermWhat it means
VectorA picture made of lines and shapes. Stays sharp at any size. SVG is one.
BitmapA picture made of tiny dots (pixels). PNG and JPG. Goes blurry when you zoom in.
EngraveThe laser shades a design into the surface.
CutThe laser burns all the way through.
ScoreA thin line burned on the surface.
IslandA piece of a stencil with nothing holding it, like the middle of an O. Without help, it falls out.
BridgeA thin strip left uncut so an island stays attached.
MirrorFlipping a design left to right. Stamps need it, or the words print backwards.

Troubleshooting

The middle of my letters fell out of my stencil.
Those were islands with no bridges. Add bridges, or use a stencil font, which has them built in. Or make it an engraving or a stamp instead, since those do not need bridges.
The laser will only engrave my file, not cut it.
It is a PNG or JPG. Run it through pngtosvg.com or Inkscape to get an SVG.
My SVG has thousands of tiny specks.
The picture had a busy or gray background. Start from a cleaner black and white picture, or in Inkscape raise Speckles in Trace Bitmap.
My text will not cut.
In Inkscape, select the text, then click Path, then Object to Path. Now the letters are shapes the laser can follow.
My stamp prints a blob.
Too much paint, or lines that are too thin. Use a thinner coat, and choose a design with thicker shapes.
The paint bled under my stencil.
Tape it down flatter and dab straight down instead of brushing. Less paint on the sponge helps.
