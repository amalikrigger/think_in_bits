Circles

A circle is one idea, every point the same distance from a center, turned into one equation. This booklet shows you how to read that equation, how to rebuild it when it has been multiplied out, how to test any point against it, and how to find the tangent at a point. It ends with a real Cambridge question, marked line by line.

By the end you can

  • write a circle's equation from its center and radius
  • read the center and radius from any circle equation
  • say whether a point is inside, on or outside a circle
  • find the tangent to a circle at a point

You need

  • distance, gradient and perpendicular gradient (Booklet 1, Straight lines)
  • completing the square (1.1 Quadratics)

How to use it

Read in order. When a box asks you to predict, decide before you click. Do each Check yourself question on paper first, then mark yourself. Your progress is saved in this browser.

A circle is a distance

Where the equation comes from, so you never have to memorize it blind.

The idea

A circle is every point that is the same distance from one fixed point. The fixed point is the center. The distance is the radius.

Warm upBooklet 1 skills

\(C(1, -2)\) and \(P(4, 4)\). Find the distance \(CP\) in exact form, the gradient of \(CP\), and the gradient of a line perpendicular to \(CP\).

distance\(\sqrt{(4 - 1)^2 + (4 - (-2))^2} = \sqrt{9 + 36} = \sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt{5}\)
gradient\(\dfrac{4 - (-2)}{4 - 1} = \dfrac{6}{3} = 2\)
perpendicular gradient: flip it and change the sign\(-\tfrac{1}{2}\),  because \(2 \times \left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = -1\)

Keep these three answers. The distance is about to become a radius, and the perpendicular gradient becomes a tangent in section 6.

Every point \((x, y)\) on this circle is \(\sqrt{45}\) from \(C(1, -2)\). Write that down as an equation.

across is \(x - 1\), up is \(y - (-2) = y + 2\)\(\sqrt{(x - 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2} = \sqrt{45}\)
square both sides\((x - 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 45\)
check with \(P(4, 4)\)\(3^2 + 6^2 = 9 + 36 = 45\).  It works.

That is the whole idea: the circle's equation is the distance formula with the square root squared away.

The equation of a circle

\((x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2\)  has center \((a, b)\) and radius \(r\)

This is not in the formula booklet (MF19). Learn it from the picture above, so you can always rebuild it.

Reading center and radius

Two rules, and the three mistakes Cambridge examiners see most.

The two rules

Center: the values of \(x\) and \(y\) that make each bracket zero. In \((x + 3)^2\), \(x + 3 = 0\) gives \(x = -3\). So the signs look flipped.

Radius: the square root of the right-hand side. The right-hand side is \(r^2\), not \(r\).

center

What is the center of \((x + 3)^2 + (y - 5)^2 = 49\)?

\(x + 3 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -3\),  \(y - 5 = 0 \Rightarrow y = 5\).  Center \((-3, 5)\), radius \(\sqrt{49} = 7\).

radius

What is the radius of \((x - 2)^2 + y^2 = 20\)?

\(r = \sqrt{20} = \sqrt{4}\sqrt{5} = 2\sqrt{5}\).  And \(y^2\) is \((y - 0)^2\), so the center is \((2, 0)\).

Going the other way

A circle has center \((2, -5)\) and radius 3. Put the center into the brackets with the signs flipped, and square the radius:

\((x - 2)^2 + (y + 5)^2 = 9\)

3

The expanded form

When the brackets have been multiplied out, complete the square to get them back.

Multiply out \((x + 5)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 36\) and you get \(x^2 + 10x + 25 + y^2 - 4y + 4 = 36\), which tidies up to

\(x^2 + y^2 + 10x - 4y - 7 = 0\)

Same circle, but you can no longer read the center. Cambridge often gives circles this way. Here are two routes back.

Route 1 · complete the square (always works)

group the \(x\) terms and the \(y\) terms\((x^2 + 10x) + (y^2 - 4y) - 7 = 0\)
half of 10 is 5, half of \(-4\) is \(-2\)\((x + 5)^2 - 25 + (y - 2)^2 - 4 - 7 = 0\)

Why subtract 25?

\((x + 5)^2 = x^2 + 10x + 25\). That is 25 more than \(x^2 + 10x\), so you take the 25 back off. Same for \((y - 2)^2\) and the 4. This is the same move as completing the square in 1.1.

move the numbers to the right: \(25 + 4 + 7 = 36\)\((x + 5)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 36\)
center \((-5, 2)\), radius \(6\)

Route 2 · Route 1 with letters

Write any expanded circle as \(x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0\). The 2 is there because half of \(2g\) is just \(g\). Now do Route 1 once, with letters on the left and our numbers on the right (\(g = 5\), \(f = -2\), \(c = -7\)).

Any circleThis circle 1 · group
\((x^2 + 2gx) + (y^2 + 2fy) + c = 0\)
\((x^2 + 10x) + (y^2 - 4y) - 7 = 0\)
2 · complete each square: half of \(2g\) is \(g\), then take \(g^2\) back off
\((x + g)^2 - g^2 + (y + f)^2 - f^2 + c = 0\)
\((x + 5)^2 - 25 + (y - 2)^2 - 4 - 7 = 0\)
3 · move the numbers to the right
\((x + g)^2 + (y + f)^2 = g^2 + f^2 - c\)
\((x + 5)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 36\)
4 · read it like section 2: make each bracket zero, square root the right
center \((-g, -f)\),  radius \(\sqrt{g^2 + f^2 - c}\)
center \((-5, 2)\),  radius \(6\)

Which route?

Write Route 1 in an exam. It always works and shows every step for the method mark. Use Route 2 as a fast check. It only works once \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) have coefficient 1, and it is not in the formula booklet.

center

What is the center of \(x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 8y = 0\)?

\((x - 3)^2 - 9 + (y + 4)^2 - 16 = 0 \Rightarrow (x - 3)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 25\).  Center \((3, -4)\), radius 5. No number term means \(c = 0\), and that is fine. This circle passes through the origin.
4

Is it really a circle?

Look before you start: is the equation ready, and could it be a circle at all?

Check 1 · \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) need coefficient 1

\(2x^2 + 2y^2 - 8x + 12y + 8 = 0\)
divide every term by 2 first\(x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y + 4 = 0\)
complete the square\((x - 2)^2 - 4 + (y + 3)^2 - 9 + 4 = 0\)
\((x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 9\): center \((2, -3)\), radius 3

Check 2 · the right-hand side must be positive

\(x^2 + y^2 + 2x + 4y + 10 = 0\)
\((x + 1)^2 - 1 + (y + 2)^2 - 4 + 10 = 0\)
\(1 + 4 - 10 = -5\)\((x + 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = -5\)

Two squares added together are never negative, so no point fits. This is not a circle. In Route 2 terms, \(g^2 + f^2 - c\) is negative, so there is no square root.

Why a circle has equal \(x^2\) and \(y^2\), and no \(xy\)

Expand any \((x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2\). Each bracket gives exactly one \(x^2\) or one \(y^2\), so the two always match. And \(x\) only lives in the first bracket and \(y\) in the second, so they are never multiplied together.

So \(x^2 + 2y^2 + \ldots\) is not a circle. The unequal coefficients stretch it into an oval. Anything with an \(xy\) term is tilted, so it is not a circle either.

Route check. Do not solve these.

Say what each one needs first. That is the whole task.

first move

\((x - 4)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 12\)

center \((4, -1)\), radius \(\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}\)

first move

\(x^2 + y^2 - 8x + 2y = 0\)

\((x - 4)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 17\). The same center as the last one; only the radius changed.

first move

\(3x^2 + 3y^2 + 6x - 12y - 12 = 0\)

\(x^2 + y^2 + 2x - 4y - 4 = 0 \Rightarrow (x + 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 9\)

first move

\(x^2 + 2y^2 + 4x - 6 = 0\)

The coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) differ, so this is not a circle.
5

Inside, on or outside

Put the point in, compare with \(r^2\). No square roots needed.

The test

Substitute the point into the left-hand side of \((x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2\). Less than \(r^2\): inside. Equal: on. More: outside.

Why it works

The left-hand side is the distance from the center, squared. Comparing squares is the same as comparing the distances, because distances are never negative. That is why you never need the square root.

On our circle, \((x - 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 45\)

\((5, 1)\)\((5 - 1)^2 + (1 + 2)^2 = 16 + 9 = 25\),  \(25 < 45\): inside
\((7, 1)\)\((7 - 1)^2 + (1 + 2)^2 = 36 + 9 = 45\),  \(45 = 45\): on the circle
\((-5, 2)\)\((-5 - 1)^2 + (2 + 2)^2 = 36 + 16 = 52\),  \(52 > 45\): outside

Watch the signs: \((-5 - 1)^2 = (-6)^2 = 36\). The square makes it positive. Go back to the Circle Probe in section 1 and check all three.

St. Croix · is the town inside the storm?

A made-up storm. Put Christiansted at the origin, with one grid unit = 10 km. The eye is at \((6, 8)\), and storm-force winds reach 120 km from the eye, so \(r = 12\) and the edge of the winds is \((x - 6)^2 + (y - 8)^2 = 144\).

Predict first: is Christiansted inside? Is Frederiksted, at about \((-2, -1)\)? Then use the buttons to check.

Christiansted gives \(36 + 64 = 100 < 144\): inside, 100 km from the eye. Frederiksted gives \(64 + 81 = 145 > 144\): outside, but only just (about 120.4 km). Half a kilometer decides it, which is why forecasters care about the exact radius.

The tangent at a point

A tangent is perpendicular to the radius where it touches.

The fact

The tangent at \(P\) is at right angles to the radius \(CP\). So: find the gradient of the radius, flip it and change the sign, and draw the line through \(P\).

Why a right angle?

Tilt a line through \(P\) even a little away from \(90^\circ\) and one side of it dips inside the circle, so it crosses the circle a second time. Only the line at right angles to the radius stays outside everywhere except at \(P\). The Tangent Lab below lets you see this.

Tangent to \((x - 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 45\) at \(P(4, 4)\)

1 · center from the equation\(C(1, -2)\)
2 · gradient of the radius\(\dfrac{4 - (-2)}{4 - 1} = 2\)
3 · flip and change the signtangent gradient \(-\tfrac{1}{2}\)
4 · line through \(P\), then times 2\(y - 4 = -\tfrac{1}{2}(x - 4) \Rightarrow 2y - 8 = -x + 4\)
\(x + 2y - 12 = 0\)

These are your warm-up answers from section 1: the radius gradient was 2 and the perpendicular gradient was \(-\tfrac{1}{2}\).

Try it · the Tangent Lab

Pick a point and a method. Does the line only touch, or does it cut through?

The trap

Tangent gradient \(= 2\), the radius gradient.

The radius goes into the circle through the center. The tangent only touches it. Flip and change the sign. You do not need calculus here, and the examiners say students who tried it did worse.

a special case

The circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\) passes through \((0, 5)\). What is the tangent there?

The radius from \((0, 0)\) to \((0, 5)\) is vertical, so its gradient is undefined and "flip it" has nothing to flip. Draw it: the tangent is horizontal, \(y = 5\). When a gradient is 0 or undefined, sketch first.

Exam corner

A real Cambridge question opens exactly like this booklet. Try it, then watch the marks light up.

June 2023 · Paper 11 · Question 12 (a) and (b)

The diagram shows a circle \(P\) with center \((0, 2)\) and radius 10 and the tangent to the circle at the point \(A\) with coordinates \((6, 10)\). It also shows a second circle \(Q\) with center at the point where this tangent meets the \(y\)-axis and with radius \(\tfrac{5}{2}\sqrt{5}\).

(a) Write down the equation of circle \(P\). [1]

(b) Find the equation of the tangent to the circle \(P\) at \(A\). [2]

Parts (c) and (d), where the tangent meets circle \(Q\), are in Booklet 3.

Sketch first. The center is on the \(y\)-axis and \(A\) is up and to the right of it, so the radius slopes up and the tangent must slope down. Now write (a) and (b) on paper before you go on.

(a) \(x^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 100\)
A mark for a correct statement that needs no method. Earn it with \(-2\) in the bracket and \(10^2 = 100\) on the right.
(b) \(m_{\text{radius}} = \dfrac{10 - 2}{6 - 0} = \dfrac{4}{3}\),  so \(m_{\text{tangent}} = -\dfrac{3}{4}\)
A method mark. Earn it by showing either gradient.
\(y - 10 = -\tfrac{3}{4}(x - 6)\),  so \(y = -\tfrac{3}{4}x + \tfrac{29}{2}\)
An accuracy mark, which needs the M1 before it. Any correct form scores, because this question does not ask for \(ax + by + c = 0\). In integers: \(3x + 4y - 58 = 0\).

Check: \(A(6, 10)\) must be on the answer. \(3(6) + 4(10) - 58 = 0\). It works.

Reading the codes

M is a method mark: a valid method with the numbers put in. A is an accuracy mark for the correct answer, and it needs the M before it. B is a mark for a correct fact or value that needs no method. A star and a D (as in Booklet 1) mean a later mark depends on an earlier one. This part has none.

Find the mistake

Three answers to this question. Decide what is wrong with each, then open it.

Answer A

(a) \(x^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 10\)

Test \(A(6, 10)\), which is on the circle: \(36 + 64 = 100\), not 10. The right-hand side is \(r^2\).

Answer B

(a) \(x^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 100\)

\((y + 2)\) is zero at \(y = -2\), so this circle's center is \((0, -2)\). Center \((0, 2)\) needs \((y - 2)^2\).

Answer C

(b) gradient \(\tfrac{4}{3}\), so \(y = \tfrac{4}{3}x + 2\)

Put \(x = 0\): \(y = 2\). This line goes through the center, so it is the radius. Flip and change the sign: \(-\tfrac{3}{4}\).

These are the mistakes the June 2023 examiner report describes for this question. Answer C's check, "does my tangent go through the center?", catches the error every time.

12section 6 · stretch

A radius has gradient \(m\), where \(m \ne 0\). Explain why the tangent at the end of that radius has gradient \(-\dfrac{1}{m}\).

The tangent is perpendicular to the radius. Perpendicular gradients multiply to \(-1\), so \(m \times m_{\text{tangent}} = -1\), which gives \(m_{\text{tangent}} = -\dfrac{1}{m}\). Say it in words in an exam: "the product of the gradients is \(-1\)."

9

Four things to carry with you

  1. \((x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2\) is the distance formula, squared. The center makes each bracket zero. The right-hand side is \(r^2\).
  2. Expanded form: divide first if \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) do not have coefficient 1, then complete the square on \(x\) and on \(y\). A negative right-hand side means no circle.
  3. Inside, on or outside: substitute the point and compare with \(r^2\).
  4. Tangent at a point: radius gradient, then flip and change the sign. Sketch first when a gradient is 0 or undefined.
