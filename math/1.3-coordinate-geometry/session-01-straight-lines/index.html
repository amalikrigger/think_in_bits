Do Now · 5 min · no notes, no calculator

\(A(1, 2)\) and \(B(5, 10)\). Find the gradient of \(AB\), the midpoint of \(AB\), and the length of \(AB\).

Give the length in exact form. Sketch the two points first.

Write all three, then tap to check.
gradient:  \(\dfrac{10 - 2}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{8}{4} = 2\)
midpoint:  \(\left(\dfrac{1 + 5}{2}, \dfrac{2 + 10}{2}\right) = (3, 6)\)
length:  \(\sqrt{(5 - 1)^2 + (10 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{16 + 64} = \sqrt{80} = \sqrt{16}\sqrt{5} = 4\sqrt{5}\)
Gradient \(2\), midpoint \((3, 6)\), length \(4\sqrt{5}\). A decimal like 8.94 is not exact.

Direct instruction · 1 of 9 · three measurements

Gradient, midpoint and length, from two points \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\).

Gradient

\(m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\)

Change in \(y\) divided by change in \(x\). Subtract in the same order on top and bottom.

Midpoint

\(\left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)\)

The average of the two \(x\)-coordinates, and the average of the two \(y\)-coordinates.

Length (distance)

\(\sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\)

Pythagoras on the across and up distances. Leave it as a surd unless the question asks for a decimal.

Two habits that save marks

Sketch first, every time. A rough sketch tells you whether a gradient should be positive or negative, and catches a sign slip before it costs you.

None of these three formulas is in the formula booklet (MF19). You need them from memory.

Simplify the surd. Look for a square factor: \(\sqrt{80} = \sqrt{16 \times 5} = 4\sqrt{5}\).

Direct instruction · 2 of 9 · the equation of a line

One line, three ways to write it. Work in the first, convert at the end.

The line through \((-2, 4)\) with gradient \(\tfrac{5}{3}\).

1 · Point and gradient

\(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\)
\(y - 4 = \tfrac{5}{3}(x + 2)\)

Use this when you know a point and the gradient. It is the form Cambridge's own model answers start from.

2 · Gradient and intercept

\(y = mx + c\)
expand:  \(y - 4 = \tfrac{5}{3}x + \tfrac{10}{3}\)
add 4:  \(y = \tfrac{5}{3}x + \tfrac{22}{3}\)

Use this to read off the gradient \(m\) and where the line crosses the \(y\)-axis, \(c\).

3 · \(ax + by + c = 0\)

form 1, times 3:
\(3y - 12 = 5x + 10\)
all on one side:
\(0 = 5x - 3y + 22\)
\(5x - 3y + 22 = 0\)

Read the question for the form

When a question says "in the form \(ax + by + c = 0\), where \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) are integers", multiply through to clear every fraction, and make sure the equation ends in "= 0". On a June 2024 question, about half of the students who found the right line lost the last mark because the answer was not in the form asked for.

Check it: put \((-2, 4)\) into the answer. \(5(-2) - 3(4) + 22 = -10 - 12 + 22 = 0\). It works.

Direct instruction · 3 of 9 · parallel and perpendicular

Parallel lines share a gradient. Perpendicular gradients multiply to \(-1\).

parallel:  \(m_1 = m_2\)
perpendicular:  \(m_1 m_2 = -1\),  so  \(m_2 = -\dfrac{1}{m_1}\)
flip it and change the sign:  \(\tfrac{4}{7} \to -\tfrac{7}{4}\).  Check: \(\tfrac{4}{7} \times \left(-\tfrac{7}{4}\right) = -1\)

A line given as \(ax + by + c = 0\): rearrange to \(y = mx + c\) first.

\(4x + 5y - 20 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 5y = -4x + 20 \;\Rightarrow\; y = -\tfrac{4}{5}x + 4\)
gradient \(-\tfrac{4}{5}\),  parallel gradient \(-\tfrac{4}{5}\),  perpendicular gradient \(\tfrac{5}{4}\)

To show two lines are perpendicular, say it in words: "the product of the gradients is \(-1\), so the lines are perpendicular."

Which line does this gradient belong to?

curve   line \(AB\)   tangent at \(B\)

A question tells you the gradient of the curve at \(B\) is \(-3\). That is the gradient of the tangent at \(B\), the dotted line. The line \(AB\) is a different line with a different gradient, \(-\tfrac{13}{2}\).

Some students took the gradient of the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) to be \(\tfrac{1}{3}\), straight from the \(-3\). Before you flip a gradient, ask which line it belongs to.

Direct instruction · 4 of 9 · where two lines meet

The point where two lines cross satisfies both equations, so solve them simultaneously.

Find where \(x + 2y = 4\) and \(3x - y = 19\) meet.

Substitution

make \(y\) the subject of the second:  \(y = 3x - 19\)
put it into the first:  \(x + 2(3x - 19) = 4\)
\(x + 6x - 38 = 4 \;\Rightarrow\; 7x = 42 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 6\)
\(y = 3(6) - 19 = -1\)

Elimination

double the second so the \(y\) terms cancel:  \(6x - 2y = 38\)
add it to the first:  \(x + 6x = 4 + 38 \;\Rightarrow\; 7x = 42 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 6\)
back into the first:  \(6 + 2y = 4 \;\Rightarrow\; y = -1\)
Both routes give \((6, -1)\).

Use substitution when one equation already says \(y = \ldots\) or \(x = \ldots\). Use elimination when both are in the \(ax + by = c\) shape. Check the answer in the equation you did not substitute into: \(3(6) - (-1) = 19\). It works.

Direct instruction · 5 of 9 · the perpendicular bisector

The perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) cuts \(AB\) in half, at a right angle.

line \(AB\)   perpendicular bisector

"Bisector" means it cuts \(AB\) into two equal halves, so it passes through the midpoint \(M\). "Perpendicular" means it meets \(AB\) at a right angle. So you need two things:

1 · a point:  the midpoint \(M\) of \(AB\)
2 · a gradient:  find the gradient of \(AB\), then flip it and change the sign
3 · the line:  \(y - y_M = m(x - x_M)\) with that point and that gradient
4 · the form:  convert to the form the question asks for

Every point on this line is the same distance from \(A\) as from \(B\). That fact comes back next lesson with circles.

Direct instruction · 7 of 9 · June 2024 · Paper 12 · Q10(b)

The curve is \(y = (5 - 2x)^{\frac{3}{2}} + 5\). Find the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).

The curve is defined for \(x < \tfrac{5}{2}\). Point \(A\) on the curve has \(y\)-coordinate 32. Point \(B\) on the curve is where the gradient of the curve is \(-3\).

Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\). Give your answer in the form \(ax + by + c = 0\), where \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) are integers. [6]

finding \(B\) needs calculus (later this year), so here are both points:  \(A(-2, 32)\), \(B(2, 6)\)

Check \(A\) and \(B\) are on the curve: \(x = -2\) gives \((5 + 4)^{\frac{3}{2}} + 5 = 27 + 5 = 32\), and \(x = 2\) gives \((5 - 4)^{\frac{3}{2}} + 5 = 1 + 5 = 6\).

Draw the picture first. \(A\) is up and to the left, \(B\) is down and to the right, so the gradient of \(AB\) should come out negative.

The \(-3\) is the gradient of the curve at \(B\). We do not use it in this part.

curve   \(AB\)   perpendicular bisector

The two axes use different scales, so the right angle at \(M\) does not look square here.

Direct instruction · 8 of 9 · Q10(b) · where the last 4 marks come from

Each line of working below earns one mark.

M1*  gradient of \(AB\): \(m_1 = \dfrac{32 - 6}{-2 - 2} = \dfrac{26}{-4} = -\dfrac{13}{2}\),  so the perpendicular gradient is \(-\dfrac{1}{m_1} = \dfrac{2}{13}\)1 mark
M1*  midpoint of \(AB\): \(\left(\dfrac{-2 + 2}{2}, \dfrac{32 + 6}{2}\right) = (0, 19)\)1 mark
DM1  the line through the midpoint with the perpendicular gradient: \(y - 19 = \dfrac{2}{13}(x - 0)\)1 mark
A1  multiply by 13: \(13y - 247 = 2x\),  then move everything to one side: \(2x - 13y + 247 = 0\)1 mark
\(2x - 13y + 247 = 0\)  (any integer multiple, such as \(-2x + 13y - 247 = 0\), also scores)

Reading the codes. M is a method mark, A is an accuracy mark. The star and the D go together: the DM1 depends on both starred marks, so the equation mark only counts if you used a perpendicular gradient and the midpoint. The first two of the 6 marks are for finding \(B\) with calculus.

Check: the midpoint \((0, 19)\) must be on the answer. \(2(0) - 13(19) + 247 = -247 + 247 = 0\). It works.

Spot the mistake · 3 min

Same June 2024 question, three answers. What is wrong with each? Tap to check.

\(A(-2, 32)\), \(B(2, 6)\), and the gradient of the curve at \(B\) is \(-3\). Find the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) as \(ax + by + c = 0\).
A.  "The gradient at \(B\) is \(-3\), so the bisector has gradient \(\tfrac{1}{3}\).  \(y - 19 = \tfrac{1}{3}x\), so \(x - 3y + 57 = 0\)."
which line is \(-3\) the gradient of?  the tangent at \(B\). The line \(AB\) is a different line.
Wrong gradient. The bisector is perpendicular to \(AB\), whose gradient is \(-\tfrac{13}{2}\), so it needs \(\tfrac{2}{13}\).
B.  "Gradient \(AB = -\tfrac{13}{2}\), so the bisector has gradient \(\tfrac{2}{13}\).  \(y - 32 = \tfrac{2}{13}(x + 2)\), so \(2x - 13y + 420 = 0\)."
test the midpoint \((0, 19)\):  \(2(0) - 13(19) + 420 = 173\), so the midpoint is not on this line
Wrong point. The line goes through \(A\). A bisector has to go through the midpoint.
C.  "Gradient \(\tfrac{2}{13}\) through the midpoint \((0, 19)\), so \(y = \tfrac{2}{13}x + 19\)."
right line, but read the question:  it asks for \(ax + by + c = 0\) with integers
Wrong form. It loses the A1. Multiply by 13 and move everything to one side: \(2x - 13y + 247 = 0\).

These are the three mistakes the June 2024 examiner report describes for this question.

Practice · together · 6 min

Sketch first. Give every equation as \(ax + by + c = 0\) with integers. Tap to check.

1.  \(A(-3, 1)\), \(B(5, 7)\). Find the length \(AB\), the midpoint of \(AB\), and the equation of the line \(AB\).
length:  across \(5 - (-3) = 8\), up \(7 - 1 = 6\), so \(\sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10\)
midpoint:  \(\left(\tfrac{-3 + 5}{2}, \tfrac{1 + 7}{2}\right) = (1, 4)\)
gradient:  \(\tfrac{7 - 1}{5 - (-3)} = \tfrac{6}{8} = \tfrac{3}{4}\)
\(y - 1 = \tfrac{3}{4}(x + 3) \Rightarrow 4y - 4 = 3x + 9\)
\(10\);  \((1, 4)\);  \(3x - 4y + 13 = 0\)
2.  Find the equation of the line through \((2, -1)\) perpendicular to \(2x + 3y = 6\).
rearrange:  \(3y = -2x + 6 \Rightarrow y = -\tfrac{2}{3}x + 2\), gradient \(-\tfrac{2}{3}\)
flip and change sign:  \(\tfrac{3}{2}\)
\(y + 1 = \tfrac{3}{2}(x - 2) \Rightarrow 2y + 2 = 3x - 6\)
\(3x - 2y - 8 = 0\)
3.  Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of \(P(1, 5)\) and \(Q(7, -3)\).
midpoint:  \(\left(\tfrac{1 + 7}{2}, \tfrac{5 + (-3)}{2}\right) = (4, 1)\)
gradient \(PQ\):  \(\tfrac{-3 - 5}{7 - 1} = -\tfrac{8}{6} = -\tfrac{4}{3}\), so perpendicular \(\tfrac{3}{4}\)
\(y - 1 = \tfrac{3}{4}(x - 4) \Rightarrow 4y - 4 = 3x - 12\)
\(3x - 4y - 8 = 0\)

4.  \(A(0, 1)\), \(B(4, 3)\), \(C(2, 7)\). Show that angle \(ABC\) is a right angle, and find the area of triangle \(ABC\).
gradients:  \(AB = \tfrac{3 - 1}{4 - 0} = \tfrac{1}{2}\),  \(BC = \tfrac{7 - 3}{2 - 4} = -2\)
\(\tfrac{1}{2} \times (-2) = -1\), so \(AB\) is perpendicular to \(BC\) and angle \(ABC = 90^\circ\).
lengths:  \(AB = \sqrt{16 + 4} = 2\sqrt{5}\),  \(BC = \sqrt{4 + 16} = 2\sqrt{5}\)
right angle at \(B\), so these are base and height:  \(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2\sqrt{5} \times 2\sqrt{5}\)
Area \(= 10\)
5.  The lines \(2x + y = 7\) and \(x - y = -1\) meet at \(P\). Find \(P\), then the line through \(P\) perpendicular to \(x - y = -1\).
second line:  \(y = x + 1\);  into the first: \(2x + x + 1 = 7 \Rightarrow 3x = 6 \Rightarrow x = 2\), so \(y = 2 + 1 = 3\)
gradient of \(y = x + 1\) is 1:  perpendicular gradient \(-1\)
\(y - 3 = -(x - 2) \Rightarrow y - 3 = -x + 2\)
\(P(2, 3)\);  \(x + y - 5 = 0\)
6.  \(y = 2x + 3\) meets the \(x\)-axis at \(A\) and the \(y\)-axis at \(B\). Find the length \(AB\) and the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).
\(y = 0\):  \(x = -\tfrac{3}{2}\), so \(A\left(-\tfrac{3}{2}, 0\right)\);  \(x = 0\):  \(B(0, 3)\)
\(AB = \sqrt{\tfrac{9}{4} + 9} = \sqrt{\tfrac{45}{4}} = \tfrac{3}{2}\sqrt{5}\)
midpoint  \(\left(-\tfrac{3}{4}, \tfrac{3}{2}\right)\);  \(AB\) has gradient 2, so  \(-\tfrac{1}{2}\)
\(y - \tfrac{3}{2} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\left(x + \tfrac{3}{4}\right)\);  multiply by 8: \(8y - 12 = -4x - 3\)
\(AB = \tfrac{3}{2}\sqrt{5}\);  \(4x + 8y - 9 = 0\)

Direct instruction · 9 of 9 · recap

Four things to carry out of here.

Gradient, midpoint and length are not in the formula booklet. Know them, sketch first, and leave lengths as simplified surds.

Work in \(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\), and convert at the end. "Integers" means clear every fraction and finish with "= 0".

Perpendicular: flip and change the sign, so \(m_1 m_2 = -1\). First check which line the gradient you were given belongs to.

Perpendicular bisector = midpoint + perpendicular gradient. Two pieces of information, one line.
