Why this matters · 2 min

Real shapes are rarely a single sector. They are sectors with pieces added or cut away.

Where you will see it

A windshield wiper wipes a band of glass: a big sector with a small sector cut out where the arm has no rubber.

An arched doorway or window, like the arches along the streets of Christiansted, has a curved top. The piece above the straight line is a segment.

A slice of pizza with the crust cut off is a sector minus a segment.

On the exam

Circular measure questions on recent Paper 1s were worth 4 to 10 marks each. Almost all of them are compound shapes: you are never told "find the segment"; you have to see it.

Today's method for any shaded region: name the shapes it is made of, find each one, then add or subtract.

From today on, you use sin, so the calculator mode matters again: radians.

New · the triangle inside a sector

Triangle \(OAB\) has two sides of length \(r\) with the angle \(\theta\) between them, so its area is \(\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\sin\theta\).

radii \(OA\), \(OB\)   chord \(AB\)   triangle \(OAB\)

The rule you used in the Do Now: a triangle with two sides \(a\) and \(b\) and the angle \(C\) between them has area \(\tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C\). No height needed.

In a sector: the two sides are both radii, \(a = b = r\), and the angle between them is \(\theta\).

triangle \(OAB = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\sin\theta\)

It looks like the sector formula \(\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta\), with \(\sin\theta\) in place of \(\theta\). Don't mix them up.

Your turn: \(r = 5\), \(\theta = 1\) radian. Find the triangle's area.

Tap to check.
\(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 25 \times \sin 1 = 12.5 \times 0.84147\) (radians mode)
\(10.5\) cm²

Got \(0.218\)? Your calculator is in degrees mode. Switch it.

Segment Lab · segment = sector − triangle

The segment is the sector with the triangle taken away. Drag \(\theta\) and watch all three areas.

segment area:  \(\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta - \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\sin\theta = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2(\theta - \sin\theta)\)

Try this: press the degrees-mode button. The triangle almost vanishes and the "segment" becomes nearly the whole sector. That is the mistake to watch for in every answer.

Worked example · segment area, exactly

A sector has radius 6 cm and angle \(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\). Find the area of the segment cut off by the chord, exactly and to 3 s.f.

The plan: segment = sector − triangle. Find each, then subtract.

Step 1 · the sector. Tap to check.
\(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 6^2 \times \dfrac{2\pi}{3} = 18 \times \dfrac{2\pi}{3} = 12\pi\)
Step 2 · the triangle. Tap to check.
\(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 6^2 \times \sin\dfrac{2\pi}{3} = 18 \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 9\sqrt{3}\)

\(\sin\tfrac{2\pi}{3} = \sin\tfrac{\pi}{3} = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\): an exact value from Pre-Calculus (\(\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\) is \(120^\circ\), and \(\sin 120^\circ = \sin 60^\circ\)).

Step 3 · subtract. Tap to check.
\(12\pi - 9\sqrt{3} \approx 22.1\) cm²

"Exactly" means keep \(\pi\) and \(\sqrt{3}\). Only the 3 s.f. answer uses the calculator.

Angles in radians · needed for the Cambridge questions

The angles of a triangle add to \(\pi\), and a straight line is \(\pi\).

The same facts as in degrees, with \(180^\circ\) replaced by \(\pi\):

  • angles in a triangle add to \(\pi\);
  • angles on a straight line add to \(\pi\);
  • in an isosceles triangle, the two angles opposite the equal sides are equal.

Isosceles with top angle \(\theta\): the other two angles share what is left, \(\pi - \theta\), equally.

each base angle \(= \dfrac{\pi - \theta}{2}\)

Your turn (a): the top angle is 1.2 radians. Find each base angle.

Tap to check.
\(\dfrac{\pi - 1.2}{2} = 0.971\) radians

Not \(\tfrac{180 - 1.2}{2}\): never mix degrees and radians in one line.

Your turn (b): two angles of a triangle are each 0.6 radians. Find the third.

Tap to check.
\(\pi - 0.6 - 0.6 = \pi - 1.2 = 1.94\) radians

You will need exactly this on the next slide.

Cambridge · November 2020, Paper 12, Q8 · part (a) · 3 marks

Part (a): the shaded region is the triangle with the sector taken away.

In the diagram, \(ABC\) is an isosceles triangle with \(AB = BC = r\) cm and angle \(BAC = \theta\) radians. The point \(D\) lies on \(AC\) and \(ABD\) is a sector of a circle with center \(A\).

(a) Express the area of the shaded region in terms of \(r\) and \(\theta\). [3]

arc \(BD\) (center \(A\))   shaded region

Mistakes the examiners saw: using only half of triangle \(ABC\); writing \(\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\sin\theta\) for the triangle (wrong angle: \(\theta\) is not between the two sides of length \(r\)).

Which shapes make it? Tap.
shaded = triangle \(ABC\) − sector \(ABD\)
Angle \(ABC\) (between the two \(r\) sides)? Tap.
\(AB = BC\), so angle \(C\) = angle \(A = \theta\) (opposite the equal sides);
angle \(ABC = \pi - \theta - \theta = \pi - 2\theta\)
Triangle and sector. Tap.
triangle \(= \tfrac{1}{2} \times r \times r \times \sin(\pi - 2\theta)\)
sector \(ABD\) (radius \(AB = r\), angle \(\theta\)) \(= \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta\)
Subtract. Tap to check.
\(\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\sin(\pi - 2\theta) - \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta\)

\(\sin(\pi - 2\theta) = \sin 2\theta\), so \(\tfrac{1}{2}r^2(\sin 2\theta - \theta)\) is the same answer.

Cambridge · November 2020, Paper 12, Q8 · part (b) · 4 marks

Part (b): in the case where \(r = 10\) and \(\theta = 0.6\), find the perimeter of the shaded region.

Which edges go round the shaded region? Tap.
arc \(BD\) + straight \(DC\) + straight \(CB\)

\(CB = r = 10\). \(DC = AC - AD = AC - 10\), because \(AD\) is a radius of the sector.

Arc \(BD\). Tap to check.
\(r\theta = 10 \times 0.6 = 6\)

From the Cambridge sample scripts: one student lost the last mark by rounding \(10\cos 0.6\) to 8.3 and getting 22.6. Another used 0.6 instead of \(\pi - 1.2\) in the cosine rule and lost both method marks.

\(AC\): split the triangle in half (the Session 1 fold). Tap.
\(B\) to the middle \(M\) of \(AC\) makes two right triangles. In triangle \(ABM\): \(\cos 0.6 = \dfrac{AM}{10}\)
\(AM = 10\cos 0.6 = 8.2534\),  \(AC = 2 \times 8.2534 = 16.5067\)
Or by the cosine rule. Which angle? Tap.
the angle opposite \(AC\) is angle \(B = \pi - 1.2\), not 0.6
\(AC^2 = 10^2 + 10^2 - 2(10)(10)\cos(\pi - 1.2)\),  \(AC = 16.5067\)
Add the three edges. Tap to check.
\(6 + (16.5067 - 10) + 10 = 22.5067\)
perimeter \(= 22.5\) cm

Cambridge · Specimen Paper 1, Q9 · 7 marks

Two circles. The shaded region is half the small circle with a segment of the big circle taken away.

The diagram shows a circle with center \(A\) and radius \(r\). Diameters \(CAD\) and \(BAE\) are perpendicular to each other. A larger circle has center \(B\) and passes through \(C\) and \(D\).

(a) Show that the radius of the larger circle is \(r\sqrt{2}\). [1]   (b) Find the area of the shaded region in terms of \(r\). [6]

The plan: shaded = right half of the small circle − the part of it inside the big circle, which is the big circle's segment on the chord \(CD\).

(a) Tap to check.
triangle \(BAC\) has a right angle at \(A\): \(BC^2 = r^2 + r^2 = 2r^2\)
\(BC = r\sqrt{2}\)  B1
(b) Angle \(CBD\)? Tap to check.
triangle \(BAC\) is right-angled with \(AB = AC\), so angle \(ABC = \tfrac{\pi}{4}\); by symmetry angle \(ABD = \tfrac{\pi}{4}\) too
angle \(CBD = \tfrac{\pi}{2}\)
Segment of the big circle cut off by \(CD\). Tap.
M1 sector: \(\tfrac{1}{2}(r\sqrt{2})^2 \cdot \tfrac{\pi}{2} = \tfrac{1}{2}\pi r^2\)   M1 triangle: \(\tfrac{1}{2}(r\sqrt{2})^2 \sin\tfrac{\pi}{2} = r^2\)
A1 segment: \(\tfrac{1}{2}\pi r^2 - r^2\)
The shaded region. Tap to check.
B1 half the small circle: \(\tfrac{1}{2}\pi r^2\)
M1 subtract: \(\tfrac{1}{2}\pi r^2 - \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\pi r^2 - r^2\right)\)
shaded area \(= r^2\)  A1

Recap and homework · 2 min

Name the shapes, find each one, then add or subtract.

Triangle in a sector: \(\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\sin\theta\) (from \(\tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C\), calculator in radians).

Segment: sector − triangle \(= \tfrac{1}{2}r^2(\theta - \sin\theta)\). Its perimeter is arc + chord.

Angles: a triangle's angles add to \(\pi\). Use the angle between the two sides in \(\tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C\), and the angle opposite the side you want in the cosine rule.
