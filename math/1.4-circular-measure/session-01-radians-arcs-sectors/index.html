Why this matters · 2 min

Anything that turns sweeps out an arc and a sector.

Where you will see it

A lawn sprinkler that turns through an angle waters a sector. Its area is the grass that gets wet.

A lighthouse beam sweeps a sector of sea. The arc at the edge is how far the light travels along the horizon.

A bicycle wheel that turns through an angle rolls the bike forward one arc length. One full turn moves it one circumference.

A slice of pizza is a sector. The crust is the arc.

Why radians

In radians, the arc is just the radius times the angle: \(s = r\theta\). In degrees the same arc is \(\tfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r\), with the clutter built in.

From Trigonometry (starting November 9) to calculus, Cambridge expects radians whenever an angle has no degree sign.

The formula sheet (MF19) gives today's two formulas, each with the note θ in radians. That note is where marks are lost.

Recap from Pre-Calculus · radians

One radian is the angle at the center when the arc is exactly one radius long.

Why a full turn is \(2\pi\). The circumference is \(2\pi r\), which is \(2\pi\) radii (about 6.28). Each radius of arc is one radian, so a full turn is \(2\pi\) radians \(= 360^\circ\). Halve it: \(\pi\) radians \(= 180^\circ\).

Converting. Degrees to radians: multiply by \(\tfrac{\pi}{180}\). Radians to degrees: multiply by \(\tfrac{180}{\pi}\). Keep \(\pi\) in the answer (exact) when the angle is a neat fraction of \(180^\circ\); otherwise give a decimal.

Check · 4 min · together

Convert each angle. Then check your calculator's mode.

(a) \(135^\circ\) in radians, exactly
\(135 \times \dfrac{\pi}{180} = \dfrac{135}{180}\pi = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}\)
(b) \(20^\circ\) in radians, exactly
\(\dfrac{20}{180}\pi = \dfrac{\pi}{9}\)
(c) \(\dfrac{5\pi}{12}\) in degrees
\(\dfrac{5\pi}{12} \times \dfrac{180}{\pi} = \dfrac{5 \times 180}{12} = 75^\circ\)
(d) \(2.5\) radians in degrees, to 1 decimal place
\(2.5 \times \dfrac{180}{\pi} = 143.2^\circ\)

Your calculator

Every calculator has a degrees mode and a radians mode. Look for D or R (or DEG, RAD) on the screen.

The 5-second test: work out \(\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi}{6}\right)\). In radians mode you get \(0.5\). If you get \(0.00914\), you are in degrees mode: switch.

When it matters: only for \(\sin\), \(\cos\) and \(\tan\). Today's two formulas only multiply, so the mode makes no difference to them: the angle you type in must already be in radians.

No unit written? An angle like \(\theta = 1.2\) with no degree sign is in radians.

New · arc length and sector area

A sector is a fraction of the whole circle, and that fraction is \(\dfrac{\theta}{2\pi}\).

radii   arc \(s\)   angle \(\theta\)

A sector is the slice between two radii and the curved edge, like a slice of pizza. The curved edge is the arc. \(\theta\) is the angle at the center, in radians.

The fraction: a full turn is \(2\pi\) radians, so a sector with angle \(\theta\) is \(\tfrac{\theta}{2\pi}\) of the circle. Take that fraction of the circumference, then of the area.

arc: \(\tfrac{\theta}{2\pi}\) of the circumference \(2\pi r\), and the \(2\pi\) cancels
\(s = \dfrac{\theta}{2\pi} \times 2\pi r\)
\(s = r\theta\)
area: \(\tfrac{\theta}{2\pi}\) of the area \(\pi r^2\); the \(\pi\) cancels and \(\tfrac{1}{2}\) is left
\(A = \dfrac{\theta}{2\pi} \times \pi r^2\)
\(A = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta\)

Check with a half circle: \(\theta = \pi\) gives \(s = \pi r\), half of \(2\pi r\), and \(A = \tfrac{1}{2}\pi r^2\), half of \(\pi r^2\). Both right.

Sector Lab · see the formulas move

Drag \(r\) and \(\theta\). Then press the degrees button and watch the area break.

Why degrees fail. The formulas were built with \(2\pi\) for a full turn. Put in \(120\) for \(\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\) and the formula thinks the sector turns through 120 radians: about 19 full turns.

Cambridge's list of common mistakes on a sector question: using \(180\) instead of \(\pi\) in formulas that only work in radians. If a question gives degrees, convert first.

Worked example · forwards

A sector has radius 9 cm and angle \(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\). Find its arc length, area and perimeter.

Exact means leave \(\pi\) in. To 3 s.f. (3 significant figures) means press the \(\pi\) key only at the end, then round.

arc:  \(s = r\theta = 9 \times \dfrac{2\pi}{3} = 6\pi\)
\(s = 6\pi \approx 18.8\) cm
area:  \(A = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 81 \times \dfrac{2\pi}{3} = \dfrac{162\pi}{6} = 27\pi\)
\(A = 27\pi \approx 84.8\) cm²

Perimeter means every edge. A sector has three: two straight radii and the arc. The arc alone is not the perimeter.

perimeter:  \(9 + 9 + 6\pi\)
\(18 + 6\pi \approx 36.8\) cm

Worked example · backwards

A sector has perimeter 24 cm and area 20 cm². Find \(r\) and \(\theta\).

Two facts give two equations in the two unknowns:

perimeter:  \(2r + r\theta = 24\)  (1)    area:  \(\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta = 20\)  (2)

The plan: \(\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta\) is \(\tfrac{1}{2}r \times (r\theta)\), so (2) contains \(r\theta\). Get \(r\theta\) from (1) and put it in. That leaves one unknown.

from (1):  \(r\theta = 24 - 2r\)
into (2):  \(\tfrac{1}{2}r(24 - 2r) = 20\),  so \(12r - r^2 = 20\)
rearrange and factor:
\(r^2 - 12r + 20 = 0\),  \((r - 2)(r - 10) = 0\)
so  \(r = 2\)  or  \(r = 10\)
\(\theta\) for each, from \(\theta = \tfrac{24 - 2r}{r}\):
\(r = 2\): \(\theta = \dfrac{20}{2} = 10\)    \(r = 10\): \(\theta = \dfrac{4}{10} = 0.4\)

Do both work? A full turn is \(2\pi \approx 6.28\) radians. \(\theta = 10\) is more than a full turn, so no sector has that angle. Reject \(r = 2\).

\(r = 10\) cm, \(\theta = 0.4\) radians
check:  perimeter \(20 + 10(0.4) = 24\) ✓
area \(\tfrac{1}{2}(100)(0.4) = 20\) ✓

Check every pair; don't assume one fails. In some questions both pairs make real sectors and both count.

Cambridge · June 2023, Paper 11, Q4 · 4 marks

Sector area \(\dfrac{16}{3}\pi\) cm², radius 8 cm. Find the perimeter of the segment \(BCD\).

The diagram shows a sector \(ABC\) of a circle with center \(A\) and radius 8 cm. The area of the sector is \(\tfrac{16}{3}\pi\) cm². The point \(D\) lies on the arc \(BC\). Find the perimeter of the segment \(BCD\). [4]

arc \(BC\)   chord \(BC\)

A segment is the region between a chord and the arc. Its perimeter is the arc plus the chord.

Or the cosine rule: \(BC^2 = 8^2 + 8^2 - 2(8)(8)\cos\tfrac{\pi}{6} = 128 - 64\sqrt{3}\), so \(BC = 4.1411\). Same answer.

B1 · \(\theta\) from the area, then divide by 32:
\(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 8^2 \times \theta = \tfrac{16}{3}\pi\),  \(32\theta = \tfrac{16}{3}\pi\),  \(\theta = \tfrac{16\pi}{3 \times 32} = \tfrac{\pi}{6}\)
M1 · arc \(BC = r\theta\), keeping 4 or more figures:
\(8 \times \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{4\pi}{3} = 4.1888\)

The chord: triangle \(ABC\) has \(AB = AC = 8\), so it is isosceles, not right-angled. Its line of symmetry splits it into two right triangles, each with angle \(\tfrac{\pi}{12}\) at \(A\) and hypotenuse 8. Half of \(BC\) is opposite that angle.

M1 · half \(BC = 8\sin\tfrac{\pi}{12}\), so (calculator in radians mode)
\(BC = 16\sin\dfrac{\pi}{12} = 4.1411\)
A1 · perimeter \(= 4.1888 + 4.1411 = 8.33\) cm

What the examiners saw: some found the area of the segment instead of its perimeter. Others wrote \(BC = 8\tan\theta\), as if \(ABC\) had a right angle. It doesn't.

Practice together · 7 min

Try each one on paper first, then tap to check.

1. A sector has radius 5 cm and arc length 7 cm. Find the angle and the area.

Tap to check.
\(s = r\theta\), so \(7 = 5\theta\):  \(\theta = \tfrac{7}{5} = 1.4\) radians
area:  \(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 5^2 \times 1.4 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 25 \times 1.4\)
\(\theta = 1.4\),  area \(= 17.5\) cm²

Shortcut: \(\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta = \tfrac{1}{2}r(r\theta) = \tfrac{1}{2}rs = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 7 = 17.5\).

2. A sector has angle \(\tfrac{5\pi}{6}\) and area \(15\pi\) cm². Find the radius and the perimeter, exactly.

Tap to check.
area:  \(\tfrac{1}{2}r^2 \times \dfrac{5\pi}{6} = 15\pi\),  so \(\dfrac{5\pi}{12}r^2 = 15\pi\)
multiply both sides by \(\tfrac{12}{5\pi}\):
\(r^2 = 36\),  \(r = 6\) (a length, so not \(-6\))
arc:  \(6 \times \dfrac{5\pi}{6} = 5\pi\)
\(r = 6\) cm,  perimeter \(= 12 + 5\pi\) cm

Recap and homework · 3 min

Two formulas, one rule about the angle.

Radians: \(\pi\) radians \(= 180^\circ\). Multiply by \(\tfrac{\pi}{180}\) to get radians, by \(\tfrac{180}{\pi}\) to get degrees.

Arc and area: \(s = r\theta\) and \(A = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta\), with \(\theta\) in radians. Perimeter of a sector: \(2r + r\theta\). Perimeter of a segment: arc plus chord.

Backwards: two facts give two equations. Get \(r\theta\) from one and substitute; solve on the page; check every answer makes a real sector.
