1 · What a derivative isPaper 1 · understand it
The ideagradient of a chord, as the chord shrinks
Notationfirst derivative   second derivative the gradient function, then differentiate again

In words: the derivative is the gradient of the curve at one point. Take the chord between two points on the curve and slide the second point in toward the first; the chord’s gradient settles on the gradient of the curve. Cambridge expects an informal understanding of this. You will not be asked to differentiate from first principles. Cambridge uses all four symbols, so learn to read all of them.

MF19 page 3 prints the power rule as the first row of its table. It does not print the chain rule, so the chain rule is the one to memorize. Say it in words: bring the power down, take one off it, then multiply by the derivative of the inside.

3 · Powers and roots: rewrite, then differentiatePaper 1

Every one of these is the power rule. Rewrite roots and fractions as powers first, and watch the denominator: in 5/(2x⁴) the 2 stays on the bottom, so it becomes ⁵⁄₂x⁻⁴. For a negative power, taking one off goes further from zero: −2 − 1 = −3.

What changed from the Sep 14 sheet. If you still have it, these are the three corrections.

How to learn these. Page 1, sections 2 and 3 first, until they are automatic. Write them from memory on a blank page every day for a week; reading feels like learning, and writing is what sticks. Say each rule in words before you use the symbols. Then test yourself backwards: cover the right-hand column and read the left, then cover the left. The Derivative Practice tool on the class site drills exactly this.
Self-test. Cover the right-hand columns and write these from memory: the four notations, the constant, multiple and sum rules, the power rule, the chain rule in both forms, the derivatives of √x, 1/x, 1/x² and (ax + b)ⁿ. The first page pays; this page is for later.
