Do Now · 5 min · no notes, no calculator

Solve each one.

x2 − 5x + 6 = 0
(x − 2)(x − 3) = 0  →  x = 2 or x = 3
x2 − 9 = 0
(x − 3)(x + 3) = 0  →  x = 3 or x = −3
x2 + 4x = 0
x(x + 4) = 0  →  x = 0 or x = −4

Two of these lose a root if you rush. x2 − 9 loses the −3 if you only take the positive square root. x2 + 4x loses x = 0 if you divide both sides by x. Quadratics come in pairs.

Direct instruction · 1 of 12

Three ways to solve a quadratic. Pick the cheap one.

  • Factor. First choice. Ten seconds when the numbers are small and whole.
  • Complete the square. When you want the vertex, the highest or lowest point on the curve, or when the question asks for that form.
  • Formula. Always works, slowest. Use it when the first two do not apply.

Khan Academy drills all three. The skill today is picking the right one.

Direct instruction · 2 of 12

Completing the square: halve, then correct.

x2 − 6x + 5
(x − 3)2 − 9 + 5  =  (x − 3)2 − 4

Halve the −6 to get −3. Now check what you built: (x − 3)2 expands to x2 − 6x + 9. That is 9 more than you wanted, so subtract 9 to put it back. Then combine with the 5.

ax2 + bx + c ≡ a(x + p)2 + q

The ≡ sign means "is identical to". It is not asking you to solve anything. It says these two are the same expression written two ways, for every value of x.

Squaring anything gives zero or more, never less. So (x + p)2 is at its smallest when it is exactly zero, which happens when x + p = 0, so x = −p. At that x nothing is left but q. That point (−p, q) is the vertex.

Direct instruction · 3 of 12

When a ≠ 1 there are two routes. Both are right.

3x2 + 4x − 15 = 0

Route 1 · factor the a out

Pull the 3 out of the x2 and x terms. Leave the −15 outside.

3(x2 + 4/3 x) − 15 = 0

Half of 4/3 is 2/3. Square it, 4/9, and subtract it inside.

3[(x + 2/3)2 − 4/9] − 15 = 0

Multiply back out. The 4/9 sits inside, so it becomes 4/3.

3(x + 2/3)2 − 49/3 = 0

Add 49/3 to both sides, then divide by 3.

(x + 2/3)2 = 49/9

Route 2 · divide the whole line by a

Divide every term by 3. The other side is 0, and 0 ÷ 3 is still 0.

x2 + 4/3 x − 5 = 0

Move the −5 across.

x2 + 4/3 x = 5

Half of 4/3 is 2/3. Square it, 4/9, and add it to both sides.

(x + 2/3)2 = 5 + 4/9

Tidy the right side.

(x + 2/3)2 = 49/9
x + 2/3 = ± 7/3  →  x = 5/3 or x = −3

Same bracket, same answer. Route 2 is shorter here only because the other side is 0, so dividing costs nothing. On a question like this one, divide.

Two Routes, One Answer: run any quadratic down both at once

Direct instruction · 4 of 12

No equals sign? Then dividing needs one more step.

wkshtA and wkshtC never say "= 0". They say write in the form A(x + B)2 + C. There is no other side to divide into, and setting it equal to zero yourself just solves for x instead.

2x2 − 12x + 5

Route 1 · factor the a out

2(x2 − 6x) + 5
2[(x − 3)2 − 9] + 5
2(x − 3)2 − 18 + 5
2(x − 3)2 − 13

Route 2 · divide, then put the a back

x2 − 6x + 5/2
(x − 3)2 − 9 + 5/2
(x − 3)2 − 13/2  not finished
× 2  →  2(x − 3)2 − 13

Same trap on both routes, and it is the a: Route 1 brings the −9 back out as −9 instead of −18, Route 2 stops one line early. The bracket survives either way, so x = 3 is right. The minimum value is not: it is −13.

So the whole thing comes down to one question, asked before you start. Next slide.

Direct instruction · 5 of 12

Which route? Read the question first.

You do not choose by looking at the quadratic. You choose by looking at what is being asked. One thing decides it.

If there is an = 0

divide by a

It looks like one of these:

  • Solve 3x2 + 4x − 15 = 0
  • Find the roots of 2x2 − 7x + 3 = 0
  • Find the values of k for which x2 + kx + 9 = 0 has a repeated root
  • Find where the line meets the curve

The other side is 0, so dividing is free. You never need the a again.

If there is no equals sign

factor a out

It looks like one of these:

  • Express 3x2 + 4x − 15 in the form a(x + p)2 + q
  • Write 2x2 − 12x + 5 as A(x + B)2 + C
  • Find the vertex, or the turning point
  • Find the minimum value, or the maximum value
  • State the range

The a stays on the page the whole way, so there is nothing to put back.

The right-hand list is one question wearing five hats. Vertex, turning point, minimum, maximum, range are all read straight off a(x + p)2 + q, which is why every one of them needs the a to be right.

What the vertex tells you.

a > 0, opens up
vertex is the minimum
a < 0, opens down
vertex is the maximum
1The turning point.(−p, q)
2Lowest point if a > 0. Highest if a < 0.sign of a

4The range, meaning every y-value the curve can reach.y ≥ q if a > 0

The vertex does not tell you which way it opens. The sign of a does that.

To sketch any quadratic

Which way it opens (sign of a) · the vertex · the y-intercept (put x = 0, that is c).

y = 2x2 − 12x + 5 = 2(x − 3)2 − 13

Opens up, a = 2. Vertex (3, −13). Crosses the y-axis at 5. Three facts and you have the curve.

Direct instruction · 8 of 12

Where the vertex shows up outside this room.

Physics · projectile motion

A ball's height in meters, t seconds after launch:

h = −5t2 + 20t + 1
−5(t2 − 4t) + 1
−5[(t − 2)2 − 4] + 1
−5(t − 2)2 + 20 + 1 = −5(t − 2)2 + 21

a is negative, so the vertex is the maximum. Highest point 21 m, reached at t = 2 s.

Business · maximizing profit

Daily profit in dollars from selling x units:

P = −5x2 + 300x − 1000
−5(x2 − 60x) − 1000
−5[(x − 30)2 − 900] − 1000
−5(x − 30)2 + 4500 − 1000 = −5(x − 30)2 + 3500

Maximum profit $3500, at 30 units. Sell more than that and profit falls again.

Both have a < 0, so both open downward and both vertices are maximums. That has to be true: a ball comes back down, and profit falls once you overproduce. If a question asks for a maximum and your a comes out positive, you have made a sign error. Both answers came straight out of the completed square, with no calculus.

Direct instruction · 9 of 12

First: what is a root?

A root is a value of x that makes the expression equal zero. On the graph, the roots are where the curve crosses the x-axis, because the x-axis is the line y = 0.

x = [−b ± √(b2 − 4ac)] / 2a
Δ = b2 − 4ac

The ± is what gives you two answers. Δ decides whether it can.

√Δ is a real number, so −b + √Δ and −b − √Δ differ.Δ > 0 · two roots
√0 = 0. Adding and subtracting zero give the same answer.Δ = 0 · one root
No real square root of a negative. The ± has nothing to use.Δ < 0 · none

The −b and the 2a move the answers around. Only Δ changes how many there are.

Direct instruction · 10 of 12

The three cases, drawn.

Δ > 0
two real roots
Δ = 0
one repeated root
Δ < 0
no real roots

Say "repeated root". It is the syllabus's own term and the exam expects it.

Direct instruction · 11 of 12

When does a line meet a curve?

A meeting point sits on both graphs at the same time. So it has one y-value, and both equations have to give it.

line:  y = 4
curve:  y = x2

On the line, its y is 4. On the curve, its y is x2. Same point, same y.

x2 = 4

That is what "set line = curve" means. Take the right-hand side of each equation and put an equals sign between them. The y drops out because it was the same on both.

Solve it: x = 2 or x = −2. They meet at (−2, 4) and (2, 4).

The exam version is the same two steps. y = kx − k and y = −1/(2x) both start with y, so kx − k = −1/(2x).

Direct instruction · 12 of 12

Tangency: the same three cases, with a different line.

The x-axis is just the line y = 0. It is not special. Swap it for any other line and everything carries over.

cuts twice
Δ > 0
tangent, touches once
Δ = 0
misses
Δ < 0

Same curve, y = x2. Three different lines. Make the right-hand sides equal each time, then count the solutions.

In all three, a = 1 and b = 0, so Δ = b2 − 4ac collapses to just −4c.

y = 4  →  x2 − 4 = 0  →  Δ = 16cuts at x = ±2
y = 0  →  x2 = 0  →  Δ = 0tangent at x = 0
y = −1  →  x2 + 1 = 0  →  Δ = −4misses

Making the two sides equal builds a new equation. Its roots are the meeting points, and Δ counts them.

The classic error: taking Δ of the curve alone. That answers "where does the curve cross the x-axis". Your line never entered the calculation, so it scores nothing.

Direct instruction · outside class · engineering

Satellite dish: finding the tangent line.

A dish is a parabola. A signal arriving straight on bounces off toward one focus point. To know the angle it bounces at, you need the tangent line at the spot where it hits.

dish: y = x2/4  ·  point of impact: (2, 1)
1Any line through (2, 1) with slope m.y − 1 = m(x − 2) → y = mx − 2m + 1
2Make the two right-hand sides equal.x2/4 = mx − 2m + 1
3× 4, then bring everything to one side.x2 − 4mx + 8m − 4 = 0
4a = 1, b = −4m, c = 8m − 4.Δ = 16m2 − 32m + 16
5Tangent means Δ = 0. Divide by 16.(m − 1)2 = 0 → m = 1

Tangent line: y = x − 1. Check it: x2/4 = x − 1 gives (x − 2)2 = 0, one repeated root at x = 2, exactly the point we started from.

Direct instruction · outside class · business

The highest cost a business can survive.

Revenue from selling x units is R(x) = −5x2 + 300x. Fixed cost is C. The business breaks even when revenue exactly equals cost, so profit is zero.

1Break even means revenue equals cost.−5x2 + 300x = C
2Bring to one side, then × (−1).5x2 − 300x + C = 0
3a = 5, b = −300, c = C.Δ = 90000 − 20C
C < 4500 → Δ > 0. Two break-even points, so a range of outputs makes a profit.viable
C = 4500 → Δ = 0. One break-even output, x = 30. The best you can do is zero profit.knife edge
C > 4500 → Δ < 0. No output ever breaks even, at any volume.dead

The vertex says the same thing. Completing the square gives P = −5(x − 30)2 + (4500 − C), and that vertex height hits zero at exactly C = 4500.

Worked example · 10 min · real past paper

June 2023, Paper 11, Q5 [5]

The line with equation y = kx − k, where k is a positive constant, is a tangent to the curve with equation y = −1/(2x).

Find, in either order, the value of k and the coordinates of the point where the tangent meets the curve.

Before any algebra: what does "tangent" translate to? The two sides, made equal, must have exactly one solution.

Worked example · step by step

Tangent ⇒ one solution ⇒ Δ = 0.

kx − k = −1/(2x)
× 2x:  2x(kx − k) = −1
expand:  2kx2 − 2kx = −1  →  2kx2 − 2kx + 1 = 0

a = 2k  ·  b = −2k  ·  c = 1. k is the unknown you are solving for and part of a and b.

Δ = 0:  (−2k)2 − 4(2k)(1) = 0  →  4k2 − 8k = 0
4k(k − 2) = 0  →  k = 0 or k = 2  → k = 2

Both are real answers to that equation. The question said k is a positive constant, so k = 0 is rejected. Always check your answers against the wording.

k = 2 back into the quadratic:  4x2 − 4x + 1 = 0 → (2x − 1)2 = 0 → x = ½
y = −1/(2 × ½) = −1  →  point (½, −1)

(2x − 1)2 = 0 has one repeated root, which is the algebra confirming it touches once. Mark scheme accepts a quadratic in x or in y.
