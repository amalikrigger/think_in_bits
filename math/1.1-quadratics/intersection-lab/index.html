Why two answers, and not one. A straight line usually crosses a curve in two places, so a pair of simultaneous equations usually has two answer pairs. Stopping after one x-value is half an answer, and every x owes you the matching y that sits directly above or below it on the line.

Where the discriminant comes in. Substituting the line into the curve always leaves an ordinary quadratic. Δ > 0 gives two crossings, Δ = 0 gives a tangent, and Δ < 0 means the line misses, which is the algebra's way of saying there is no real answer to find.
