Radius, tangent and gradients

The tangent at P is perpendicular to the radius CP, so its gradient is −1 divided by the gradient of CP.

Back to line meets circle

The exam method. Find the gradient of CP from the center and the point. Flip it and change the sign to get the tangent's gradient. Then use y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) through P and tidy into ax + by + c = 0.

Two quick checks. P has to satisfy the circle's equation. Substituting your tangent into the circle should leave a quadratic with Δ = 0.

Why there are usually two answers. A straight line usually crosses a curve in two places, so a pair of simultaneous equations usually has two answer pairs. Stopping after one x-value is half an answer, and every x owes you the matching y that sits directly above or below it on the line.

Where the discriminant comes in. Substituting the line into the curve always leaves an ordinary quadratic. Δ > 0 gives two crossings, Δ = 0 gives a tangent, and Δ < 0 means the line misses, which is the algebra's way of saying there is no real answer to find.
