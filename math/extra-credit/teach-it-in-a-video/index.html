Everyone has learned something from a 45-second video at some point. Now you make one. Pick one idea from class, figure out how to make it click for someone else, and film it in whatever style you want. Explaining something well is the fastest way to find out whether you actually understand it, which is also why a great video can lift your grade.

Required

The must-haves

Six things every video needs. Everything else is up to you.

  1. One idea. From the topic menu, or your own idea approved by Mon Nov 9.
  2. Correct math. Cambridge notation, exact answers or 3 significant figures, and say whether you are in degrees or radians.
  3. A worked example. At least one, start to finish, with the working shown. No jumping straight to a calculator answer.
  4. 30 seconds to 3 minutes. Vertical or wide, whatever fits your platform.
  5. The Stay Safe rules. All of them, every time. They are under Stay safe, below.
  6. A complete comment. Video type, topic, team and who did what, credits, and the AI line. There is a copy-ready template under Turn it in.
Ideas, not rules

Make it legendary

The best teaching videos rarely look like a lecture. Steal any of these, or ignore all of them.

  • Hook us in the first 3 seconds.
  • Show the mistake everyone makes, then fix it.
  • End with a challenge question for the viewer.
  • Connect it to real life: sports, music, money, games, St. Croix.
  • Tell a story, act a skit, write a song, ride a trend, race the clock.
  • Draw it, animate it, build it, act it out.
  • Work a real Cambridge question and name it, like 9709/11 June 2023 Q3.
  • If it has never been done, do it.

What decides your score is how well it teaches and how much care went into it. A phone, a laptop and a sheet of paper can beat expensive gear. You never need a phone for this: a school laptop's camera or screen recorder handles every format.

Required

Stay safe

  • First name only, or no name at all.
  • No school uniform, logo, address, or anything that shows where you live.
  • Nobody else appears or is heard unless they said yes. Your teammates count as a yes.
  • Music from your editing app's library, a royalty-free library, or none. Credit any music or images you did not make.
  • Keep it school appropriate.
Break one of these and the video comes back to you and earns nothing until it is fixed.

Posting it

  • Post it if you want: TikTok, Instagram Reel, YouTube Short, or YouTube. Posting is optional and earns nothing extra.
  • Always upload the file to Schoology too. That is the copy that gets graded. Links get deleted, set to private, or blocked on the school network.
  • The Stay Safe rules apply to anything you post, forever.
Showcase. Your video may be picked to show in class or on this site. If it is, I will ask you and a parent first, and you can say no.

Help

My file is over 512 MB

Export again at 720p instead of 1080p or 4K. A 3 minute video at 720p is well under the limit.

I do not have a phone or any social accounts

You do not need either. Record on a school laptop with its camera or screen recorder, and upload the file to Schoology. You can record in the lab during Day 1 academic time.

My video is 3 minutes and 40 seconds

Cut it. The limit is part of the challenge: a tighter video almost always teaches better. Trim the intro first.

Someone on my team is not doing anything

Talk to Mr. Krigger before the due date, not after. The comment has to say who did what, so it needs to be true.

Can I use a song I like?

Only from your editing app's built-in library, a royalty-free library, or none. Popular songs get videos muted or taken down.
