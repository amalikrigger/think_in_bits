Two Moves at Once

Direct instruction · 1 of 10 · reading the notation

Three words, one column vector, and how to read the graphs.

The column vector

\(\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}\)  means  right 3, down 2
top number is across (the \(x\)-direction):  positive is right, negative is left
bottom number is up (the \(y\)-direction):  positive is up, negative is down
so these three read:  \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\) up 3  ·  \(\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) left 2  ·  \(\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}\) right 4, down 1

It is not a fraction and there is no line between the numbers. Across first, then up.

\(y = x^2\) translated by \(\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}\)

The lowest point moves across 3, then down 2, from \((0, 0)\) to \((3, -2)\). Every other point moves the same way.

Translation

Slides the whole graph. No turning, no resizing. Described by a column vector.

Stretch

Pulls the graph away from an axis. The scale factor says how many times as far, and you must say which direction.

Reflection

Mirrors the graph in a line. You must name the line, the axis that stays fixed.

Direct instruction · 5 of 10 · reading an equation

An equation is a recipe. Read it in the order you would work it out.

\(y = 3(x + 2)^2 - 5\)  is built from \(y = x^2\). What did we do to \(x^2\), and in what order?

Dashed gray: before the move    Solid cyan: after the move

Start

\(y = x^2\)

Lowest point at \((0, 0)\).

Step 1 · Stretch

\(x^2 \to 3x^2\)

\(y\)-direction, factor 3. The lowest point stays at \((0, 0)\).

Step 2 · Translate

\(3x^2 \to 3(x + 2)^2 - 5\)

By \(\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix}\). The lowest point goes to \((-2, -5)\).

How to read it

The 3 multiplies the bracket, so it is the stretch: \(y\)-direction, factor 3.  The \(+2\) is inside the bracket, and \(x + 2 = x - (-2)\), so it moves the graph left 2.  The \(-5\) is added last, so it moves the graph down 5.

A stretch in the \(y\)-direction, factor 3, followed by a translation by \(\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix}\).

Direct instruction · 6 of 10 · the other route

Cambridge accepts either order, as long as you state the order and your numbers match it.

The same graph, \(y = 3(x + 2)^2 - 5\), built the other way: translate first, then stretch.

Dashed gray: before the move    Solid cyan: after the move

Step 1 · Translate

\(x^2 \to (x + 2)^2 - \tfrac{5}{3}\)

By \(\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ -\frac{5}{3} \end{pmatrix}\). The lowest point goes to \((-2, -\tfrac{5}{3})\).

Step 2 · Stretch

\((x + 2)^2 - \tfrac{5}{3} \to 3(x + 2)^2 - 5\)

\(y\)-direction, factor 3. The \(-\tfrac{5}{3}\) is tripled to \(-5\).

Same final graph

\(y = 3(x + 2)^2 - 5\)

Same graph as the last slide, different recipe.

Why \(-\tfrac{5}{3}\) and not \(-5\)

The stretch comes second, so it triples whatever shift came first. We need the shift to end at \(-5\). So we start with \(-5 \div 3 = -\tfrac{5}{3}\).

What you can write

Route 1: a stretch in the \(y\)-direction, factor 3, then a translation by \(\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix}\).  Route 2: a translation by \(\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ -\frac{5}{3} \end{pmatrix}\), then a stretch in the \(y\)-direction, factor 3. The June 2024 mark scheme for the question on slide 12 lists both routes.

Direct instruction · 8 of 10 · June 2024 · Paper 12 · Q2

\(y = x^2\) becomes \(y = 4(x - 3)^2 + 8\). Describe fully a sequence of transformations, making clear the order. [5]

1 · read the target, multiply before add:  \(y = 4\cdot(x - 3)^2 + 8\)
2 · stretch first, the 4:  \(x^2 \to 4x^2\)
3 · then translate by \(\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}\):  \(4x^2 \to 4(x - 3)^2 + 8\)
A stretch in the \(y\)-direction, factor 4, followed by a translation by \(\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}\).

Gray dashed: the start. Amber: after the stretch. Cyan: the final curve.

The other valid route: translate by \(\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}\) first, giving \((x - 3)^2 + 2\). Then stretch in the \(y\)-direction, factor 4, giving \(4(x - 3)^2 + 8\). The shift is 2, not 8, because the stretch afterwards multiplies it by 4.

Direct instruction · 9 of 10 · where the 5 marks come from

Five separate facts, and the order is one of them.

B1  a stretch, factor 4, in the \(y\)-direction1 mark
B2  a translation with 3 in the \(x\)-direction and 8 in the \(y\)-direction (B1 if one of those three facts is missing)2 marks
M1  two translations, one in each direction, and one stretch, with nothing extra1 mark
A1  the order is correct and clear: the stretch comes before the vertical translation1 mark

What Cambridge says about answers like this

The June 2024 report gave full marks to answers as short as one sentence, and stresses correct terminology and that "the required order is clear". Two sentences is plenty. You do not need a diagram.

Spot the mistake · 4 min

Target: \(y = x^2 \to y = 4(x - 3)^2 + 8\). Three answers. What is wrong with each? Tap to check.

A.  "A translation by \(\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}\), then a stretch in the \(y\)-direction, factor 4."
follow it through:  \(x^2 \to (x - 3)^2 + 8 \to 4(x - 3)^2 + 32\)
Wrong order. The 8 got multiplied by 4, so it gives +32, and the target has +8.
B.  "A stretch of factor 4 and a translation by \(\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}\)."
what is missing:  which direction is the stretch in, and which comes first?
No direction and no order. It loses the stretch mark and the order mark.
C.  "A stretch in the \(y\)-direction, factor 4, then a translation by \(\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}\)."
check the sign:  \(\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}\) gives \(4(x + 3)^2 + 8\)
Sign error. \((x - 3)\) means right 3, so the vector has +3.

Direct instruction · 10 of 10 · recap

Three things to carry out of here.

Order matters only when both moves act on the same variable. A \(y\)-stretch with an \(x\)-translation: no. A \(y\)-stretch with a \(y\)-translation, or a flip with a \(y\)-translation: yes, because the second move acts on the shift made by the first.

Read \(y = af(x - p) + q\) as stretch first, then translate by \(\begin{pmatrix} p \\ q \end{pmatrix}\). Built the other way round, the vertical shift becomes \(\dfrac{q}{a}\).

A full description has four parts. The type, the size, the direction or axis, and the order. Write the word "then".
